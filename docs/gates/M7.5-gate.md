## 4. Debt and deviation log

| # | Item | Kind | Scheduled |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The body's height is a profile field, not a constant.** The spec's assumptions put body height in a constant until a second body exists. Landing the rule with a constant of 2 would have stranded every guard in today's one-metre storeys until claim 6 rebuilds the sites, leaving the suite red across several sessions (CLAUDE.md §4). `body_cells` in `combat_profile` (schema: 1–4, required) lets the rule land with every profile at 1 — behaviour unchanged, proven by replay — and flip to 2 in the same change as the rebuilt sites. It is also the data-driven form a second body would need. | deviation from the spec's assumptions | claim 6 flips the profiles |
| 2 | **Raid tokens do not ask the body yet.** Tokens are not actors and cross one face per opening; what a two-cell opening means to them (one edge or two) is decided with the stacked openings in claim 6. | ordering | claim 6 |
| 3 | **Rising onto a step now checks the floor over the head.** `_climbs_onto` checked the cell above for ground and pieces but never a solid floor face between them; the body rule checks it at every height. No fixture moved (no wild step runs under a built floor in any of them). | correction | closed |
| 4 | **Nothing is built where a body is — for one-cell bodies too.** Until now a foundation could be placed on someone's feet, leaving them inside it (`MovementSystem` had a rule for "an actor put inside the ground stays there"). `BuildSystem.place` now refuses a cell piece in any live actor's body cells and a solid floor between them; `region.fill` refuses any body cell, not just the feet. No fixture moved (replayed 14:39–14:44). One test built over its own player (`test_vertical_movement` placed its foundation grid on the cell the player stood in); its player now starts off the build area. So did `test_quest_system.gd::test_a_build_objective_credits_the_builder`, which placed its first foundation on its player's feet; it was missed at claim 2 because only the affected files were run, and found by the first full suite on 2026-10-02 (515 tests, 1 failed). Its player now stands beside the row it builds. | behaviour change, claim 2 | closed |
| 5 | **The property's arenas each get a fresh sim.** In one sim, arenas side by side made the portal graph flood their joint bounding box and hit `MAX_REGION_CELLS`. Not a defect of the graph (a 400 m build would be refused the same way), but worth knowing for any test that builds far apart. | test design | closed |
| 6 | **Eyes and centre are profile fields too** (`eye_mm`, `centre_mm`, 0–4 000), for item 1's reason: eyes at 1.6 m over today's one-metre walls would let every guard see over every wall until claim 6. At 0 they are the feet, exactly the old rule; claim 6 sets 1 600 and 1 000 with the bodies. | deviation from the spec's assumptions | claim 6 |
| 7 | **`can_target` now always checks a line from the eyes to the centre**, after the agent's sight check. With both heights at the feet it is the same line `can_see` already walked, so nothing moved; with real heights it is the spec's "a shot needs eyes to centre". | claim 3 | closed |
| 8 | **The run-log test failed on a fresh machine.** `test_run_log.gd` listed `user://logs/runs` before any run had made it, an engine error the unit stage's log check fails on. The Deck always had the folder, so it never showed there; the first suite on the laptop (2026-10-02) found it. The helper now reads a missing folder as no runs. Carried in from the M7 Deck run (`6c6dae4`). | correction | closed |
| 9 | **Resting on a piece is no longer a hop, so the M3 span test counts one wall more.** Under claim 5 the wall standing on a foundation is at the foundation's depth, 0, not 1, so a scrap-steel run from one foundation reaches five walls, not four; `test_span_limits_how_far_a_chain_reaches` now asserts the sixth is refused and checks the depths it implies. What rests on what is a slot list in `BuildSystem._slots_under`; the property's oracle judges it by geometry instead, and both 10 000-case properties compare every depth with it. Hand mutants: dropping any of four slots, or charging a hop for resting, fails the tests; re-queuing at an equal depth is equivalent (cost only). No fixture hash moved. | behaviour change, claim 5 | closed |
| 10 | **Support costs more per change.** `supported_set` on a 6 × 6 block two storeys high (252 pieces), laptop under WSL2, mean of 20 calls: 10.6 ms before claim 5, 15.5 ms after (it was 30.6 ms in the first draft, which re-queued settled pieces; fixed). 6.9 ms of either is the neighbour scan M3 already had. It runs once per placement or removal, not per frame, but the creator tool (claims 14–16) places pieces by hand: measure it on the Deck there. | performance, measured | claim 14, Deck |
| 11 | **The free drop is a profile field too** (`fall_free_levels`, 0–100, required), for items 1 and 6's reason: decision 5's three free metres over today's one-metre storeys would make every M6 fall free until claim 6. At 1 it is the M6 rule exactly: with the field read at a default of 1 and no content changed, all 24 fixtures reproduced their recorded hashes; adding the field to the three profiles then moved every hash through the content digest alone, re-recorded. Claim 6 sets 3 and `fall_damage_per_level` 4 000, so a two-storey drop does the 12 000 a two-level drop did at M6 (pinned now by a test profile). | deviation from the spec's assumptions | claim 6 |
